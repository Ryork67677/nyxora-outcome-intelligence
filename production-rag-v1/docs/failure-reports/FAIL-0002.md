# FAIL-0002 — retrieval finds the right document and the wrong chunk

> **Outcome (EXP-005): the diagnosis in this report was tested and largely refuted.**
> Bounding chunk size — the intervention this report proposes — rescued **zero** of the 20
> questions, and AN-003, the case analysed below, is *still* not retrievable after its
> evidence share of its chunk improved 2.9× (1.65% → 4.79%). Its real cause is vocabulary
> mismatch: every low-df term in the question is absent from the chunk holding the answer.
> Smaller chunks also actively hurt three cases by stripping co-occurring query terms.
> See [EXP-005-rechunking.md](../reports/EXP-005-rechunking.md). The prediction stated in
> "Proposed EXP-004" below was wrong, and is left unedited as the record of what was
> predicted before the measurement.

**Status:** diagnosed, proposed fix **tested and rejected** in EXP-005
**Stage that failed:** chunking / retrieval granularity (`rag_v1.chunking.chunk_document`)
**Corpus snapshot:** `snap_689e336380a054d8039dc35b2c09cd0a`
**Experiments:** EXP-000, EXP-001, EXP-002, EXP-003, all at k = 10

## Symptom

After FAIL-0001 was fixed, every retrieval configuration converged on the same
ceiling, and the shape of the failures was identical across all of them:

Both columns below count evidence spans, not cases: `spans_found / 22` versus the
same spans credited when only the correct *document* was reached.

| Configuration | span recall | **document-level recall** |
|---|---:|---:|
| EXP-000 lexical (BM25) | 0.455 (10/22) | 0.818 (18/22) |
| EXP-001 dense (LSA) | 0.318 (7/22) | 0.773 (17/22) |
| EXP-002 hybrid interleave | 0.455 (10/22) | 0.818 (18/22) |
| EXP-003 RRF (all `rrf_k`) | 0.500 (11/22) | 0.818 (18/22) |

Document-level recall counts a span as "reached" if the correct *document*
appeared in the top 10, even when the retrieved chunk did not overlap the
expected span. Of the 12 spans EXP-000 missed, **8 had the correct document in
the top 10 anyway**. Three quarters of the lexical failures are the retriever
arriving at the right page and returning the wrong part of it.

The identical 0.818 across lexical, interleave and RRF is the tell. Fusion is
reordering chunks *within an already-correct document set*; it is not finding
documents the individual retrievers missed. That caps what any fusion tuning can
buy on this corpus.

## Exact point of failure

Two distinct sub-cases, both traceable to chunk size.

**Case AN-003 — the answer is buried in an oversized chunk.** The expected
evidence is the 57-character sentence "There is a limit of 100,000 messages in a
single request." at offset 4554–4611. It lives inside:

```
chunk_id   chk_25a87141c0457994cd6f5d8d0cc2dd6d7683133c
section    {Batches, "Create a Message Batch", "Body Parameters"}
span       4149 – 7598   (3,449 characters)
```

The answering sentence is 1.7% of its own chunk. The rest is an unrelated dump of
every other body parameter. Neither retriever surfaces it — the correct chunk is
**not in the top 200** for lexical or dense. BM25 length normalization makes this
strictly worse, since the chunk is penalised for the 3,392 characters that have
nothing to do with the question.

This is structural, not incidental. Across the Anthropic half of the corpus:

```
avg chunk 1,207 chars | max 12,341 chars | 2,521 of 12,028 chunks over 3,000 chars
```

The chunker splits on paragraph boundaries under a 3,500-character budget, but it
never splits a single oversized paragraph, table or code block — hence chunks of
12,341 characters despite `MAX_CHUNK_CHARS=3500`. API reference pages are exactly
the shape that breaks this: enormous parameter tables in one flat section.

**Case AN-002 — the answer is retrievable but ranked below k.** The expected span
is in the `HTTP errors` section. Its rank in each retriever's candidate list:

```
lexical rank 27   dense rank 40   RRF rank 22   (of 200 candidates)
```

Present, found, ranked too low. Raising k would "fix" this case without fixing
anything real, which is why k was held at 10 across every experiment.

## Why it is not being fixed in V1

`docs/architecture/V1_SCOPE.md` puts chunking experiments at EXP-004+, after the
baseline produces real failure data. This report *is* that data. Fixing it now
would mean changing chunking and re-running every experiment against a corpus
whose measurements were produced under a different chunker — exactly the
invalidation that anchoring ground truth to `(version_id, section_path,
char_span)` rather than `chunk_id` was designed to prevent.

Because the golden set is anchored above the chunk layer, a re-chunked corpus can
be evaluated against **the same 22 unchanged evidence spans**. That is the
experiment this report justifies, and it is the one to run next.

## Proposed EXP-004 (not run)

1. Split oversized sections at sentence or list-item boundaries so no chunk
   exceeds the configured budget, and record `parent_chunk_id` in `chunk_link`
   so the surrounding context is still reachable.
2. Re-ingest, snapshot as a *new* immutable snapshot; do not promote it to
   current unless corpus-health checks pass.
3. Re-run EXP-000 through EXP-003 unchanged against both snapshots.
4. Report the paired per-case delta. The specific prediction to test: AN-003 and
   AN-002 are rescued, and document-level recall stays at 0.818 while span recall
   moves toward it.

If span recall does **not** move toward document-level recall after re-chunking,
the hypothesis in this report is wrong and reranking becomes the next candidate —
but not before.

## What this failure does not license

* Not a reranker. A reranker cannot rescue AN-003, because the correct chunk is
  absent from the top 200 candidates entirely; reranking only reorders what
  retrieval already found.
* Not a larger k. That inflates the metric without improving the retrieval.
* Not a similarity threshold. The failures are ranking and granularity failures,
  not confidence-calibration failures.
